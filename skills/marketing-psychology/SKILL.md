# Marketing Psychology & Mental Models

Apply psychological principles and mental models to marketing: why people buy, how to influence behavior ethically, and how to make better marketing decisions.

When helping: (1) identify which models apply to the situation, (2) explain the psychology behind them, (3) give specific applications, (4) flag the ethical line.

## Ethics guardrail

Several models below sit close to manipulation. The test: does the technique help the person make a decision they'd endorse on reflection, or does it exploit a bias to extract a choice they'd regret? Scarcity that's real is information; scarcity that's fabricated is a lie. Defaults that match what most people want are helpful; opt-out subscriptions people didn't notice are not. Say so when a user's plan crosses that line.

## Foundational thinking models

**First principles** — Break problems to basic truths. Don't assume you need content marketing because competitors do; ask why, what problem it solves, whether something better exists.

**Jobs to be done** — People don't buy products, they hire them to get a job done. A drill buyer wants a hole. Frame around the job, not the specs.

**Circle of competence** — Don't chase every channel. Double down where you have genuine expertise.

**Inversion** — Instead of "how do I succeed?", ask "what would guarantee failure?" List everything that would sink the campaign, then prevent each.

**Occam's razor** — If conversions dropped, check the obvious first (broken form, page speed) before assuming complex attribution issues.

**Pareto (80/20)** — Find the 20% of channels, customers, or content driving 80% of results. Cut or reduce the rest.

**Local vs. global optima** — Optimizing email subject lines won't help if email is the wrong channel. Zoom out before zooming in.

**Theory of constraints** — Every system has one bottleneck. If the funnel converts well but traffic is low, more CRO won't help.

**Opportunity cost** — Time on a low-ROI channel is time not spent on high-ROI activity. Always compare against alternatives.

**Diminishing returns** — The 10th blog post won't match the first. Know when to diversify rather than double down.

**Second-order thinking** — A flash sale boosts revenue (first order) but may train customers to wait for discounts (second order).

**Map ≠ territory** — Your persona is a useful model; real customers are more complex. Don't confuse the dashboard with the experience.

**Probabilistic thinking** — Don't bet everything on one campaign. Plan for scenarios where the primary strategy underperforms.

**Barbell strategy** — 80% into proven channels, 20% into experimental bets. Avoid the moderate-risk, moderate-reward middle.

## Understanding buyers

**Fundamental attribution error** — When customers don't convert, examine your process before blaming them. The problem is usually situational.

**Mere exposure effect** — Familiarity breeds liking. Consistent brand presence builds preference over time.

**Availability heuristic** — People judge likelihood by how easily examples come to mind. Case studies make success feel achievable.

**Confirmation bias** — Understand what your audience already believes and align messaging. Fighting beliefs head-on rarely works.

**Lindy effect** — Proven principles (clear value props, social proof) outlast trendy tactics. Don't abandon fundamentals for fads.

**Mimetic desire** — Desire is socially contagious. Waitlists, exclusivity, and social proof trigger it.

**Sunk cost fallacy** — Know when to kill underperforming campaigns. Past spend doesn't justify future spend.

**Endowment effect** — People value things more once they own them. Free trials and freemium let customers "own" the product.

**IKEA effect** — People value what they helped build. Let customers customize or configure.

**Zero-price effect** — "Free" is psychologically different from cheap. The jump from $1 to $0 is bigger than $2 to $1.

**Hyperbolic discounting** — Emphasize immediate benefits ("Start saving time today") over future ones ("ROI in 6 months").

**Status-quo bias** — Change feels risky. Reduce switching friction: "Import your data in one click."

**Default effect** — People accept pre-selected options. Powerful, and the easiest one to abuse — see the ethics guardrail.

**Paradox of choice** — Too many options paralyze. Three pricing tiers beat seven. Recommend a "best for most."

**Goal-gradient effect** — People accelerate near a goal. Progress bars and "almost there" messaging drive completion.

**Peak-end rule** — People judge experiences by the peak and the end, not the average. Design memorable peaks and strong endings.

**Zeigarnik effect** — Unfinished tasks occupy the mind. "You're 80% done," incomplete profiles, abandoned carts.

**Pratfall effect** — Competent people become more likable with a small visible flaw. "We're not the cheapest, but..." builds trust.

**Curse of knowledge** — Your product seems obvious to you and confusing to newcomers. Test copy with people outside your space.

**Mental accounting** — People treat money differently by source and intended use. "$3/day" lands differently than "$90/month."

**Regret aversion** — Money-back guarantees, free trials, "no commitment" reduce the fear of regretting the purchase.

**Bandwagon / social proof** — Customer counts, testimonials, logos, reviews, "trending" indicators create confidence.

## Influence and persuasion

**Reciprocity** — Give first. Free content, free tools, generous free tiers create reciprocal obligation.

**Commitment & consistency** — Get small commitments first (email signup, free trial). One step taken makes the next likelier.

**Authority bias** — Expert endorsements, certifications, "featured in" logos, thought leadership.

**Liking / similarity** — Relatable spokespeople, founder stories. "Built by marketers for marketers" signals similarity.

**Unity** — Shared identity drives influence. Position the brand as part of the customer's tribe.

**Scarcity / urgency** — Limited availability raises perceived value. **Only use when genuine.**

**Foot-in-the-door** — Small request then escalate. Free trial → paid → annual → enterprise.

**Door-in-the-face** — Large request, then retreat to the real ask. Enterprise pricing first makes the starter plan feel like a deal.

**Loss aversion / prospect theory** — Losses feel roughly twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good. "Don't miss out" often beats "you could gain."

**Anchoring** — The first number seen shapes all later judgments. Show the higher price first.

**Decoy effect** — A third, clearly inferior option makes the preferred tier look obvious.

**Framing** — "90% success rate" and "10% failure rate" are identical facts that feel different.

**Contrast effect** — Show the "before" state clearly; the contrast makes the "after" vivid.

## Pricing psychology

**Charm pricing / left-digit effect** — $99 feels much cheaper than $100. The left digit dominates. Use for value-focused products.

**Rounded-price (fluency) effect** — Round numbers feel premium and process easier. $500/month signals quality; $497 signals value.

**Rule of 100** — Under $100, percentage discounts seem larger ("20% off"). Over $100, absolute discounts seem larger ("$50 off"). An $80 product: "20% off" beats "$16 off." A $500 product: "$100 off" beats "20% off."

**Price relativity / good-better-best** — Three tiers with the middle as your target. The expensive tier makes it reasonable; the cheap tier anchors.

**Mental accounting** — "$1/day" feels cheaper than "$30/month." "Less than your morning coffee" reframes the expense.

## Design and delivery

**Hick's law** — Decision time rises with number of choices. One clear CTA beats three. Fewer form fields beat more.

**AIDA** — Attention → Interest → Desire → Action. Capture attention before building desire.

**Rule of 7** — Roughly 7 touchpoints before converting. Build multi-touch campaigns; retargeting and email sequences compound.

**Nudge theory / choice architecture** — Defaults, ordering, and friction reduction guide behavior without restricting choice.

**BJ Fogg behavior model** — Behavior = Motivation × Ability × Prompt. All three must be present. High motivation but hard to do won't happen; easy but unprompted won't happen.

**EAST** — Make desired behaviors Easy, Attractive, Social, Timely.

**COM-B** — Behavior requires Capability, Opportunity, Motivation. Can they do it? Is the path clear? Do they want to?

**Activation energy** — Reduce starting friction. Pre-fill forms, offer templates, show quick wins. Make the first step trivial.

**North star metric** — One metric that captures the value delivered. Align all efforts toward it.

**Cobra effect** — Incentives backfire. A referral bonus might attract low-quality referrals gaming the system. Test incentive structures.

## Growth and scaling

**Feedback loops** — Build virtuous cycles: more users → more content → better SEO → more users.

**Compounding** — Consistent content, SEO, and brand building compound. Early gains matter most.

**Network effects** — Design features that improve with more users: shared workspaces, integrations, marketplaces, communities.

**Flywheel** — Content → traffic → leads → customers → case studies → more content. Hard to start, easy to maintain.

**Switching costs** — Integrations, accumulated data, workflow customization, team adoption. Raise them ethically.

**Exploration vs. exploitation** — Don't abandon working channels for shiny new ones, but allocate some budget to experiments.

**Critical mass** — Focus resources on one segment until growth self-sustains. Depth before breadth.

**Survivorship bias** — Study failed campaigns, not just successes. The viral hit you're copying had 99 invisible failures.

## Quick reference

| Challenge | Relevant models |
|-----------|-----------------|
| Low conversions | Hick's Law, Activation Energy, BJ Fogg, friction |
| Price objections | Anchoring, framing, mental accounting, loss aversion |
| Building trust | Authority, social proof, reciprocity, pratfall effect |
| Increasing urgency | Scarcity, loss aversion, Zeigarnik effect |
| Retention / churn | Endowment effect, switching costs, status-quo bias |
| Growth stalling | Theory of constraints, local vs. global optima, compounding |
| Decision paralysis | Paradox of choice, default effect, nudge theory |
| Onboarding | Goal-gradient, IKEA effect, commitment & consistency |

## Questions to ask

1. What specific behavior are you trying to influence?
2. What does your customer believe before encountering your marketing?
3. Where in the journey (awareness → consideration → decision) is this?
4. What's currently preventing the desired action?
5. Have you tested this with real customers?
